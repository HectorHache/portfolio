For over 10 years my job was making large operations predictable. Workforce planning across call centers with 10.000+ agents at peak, at Klarna, 5CA and Microsoft. Erlang-C forecasts, shrinkage models, intraday steering, service-level recovery when a queue went sideways at 09:00. The work taught one lesson repeatedly: a system that depends on someone watching it is not a system yet.

Agents were the obvious next step. The same questions apply, only the workers changed: what is the load, where does it queue, what happens when a dependency fails, how do you know before a user tells you. So I stopped reading about it and built the thing. A household stack that orchestrates multiple agents, unifies 26 provider accounts across 14 services behind one config, serves my family through an authenticated gateway, monitors itself, and heals itself on a schedule.

What I bring is not a course certificate. It is over 10 years of capacity planning and failure analysis, pointed at LLM infrastructure, plus a running production system whose numbers I can show. I read systems for patterns and I build the boring parts first, because the boring parts are what keep running at 03:00.

03

Method

How this page was made, which is also how the stack iterates and adapts: debate, gate, build, verify, ship, maintain. The loop is the deliverable.

Fig. 2: The pipeline that produced this page. Hand-drawn and dependency-free: it loads anywhere, instantly, and has nothing that can quietly break.

01

debate.

Four AI experts argue the direction from different angles. I referee the tie.

Technical notes

Four models, four biases: Craft (claude-opus-4-8) protects taste, Market (gpt-5.6-sol) protects the reader, Ecosystem (gemini-3.8-flash) protects coherence, Contrarian (deepseek-v4-flash) kills weak ideas. Each answered the four forks in ≤300 words, then a cross-examination round: concede one point, defend your strongest claim, attack the leader's weak spot.

02

lock.

Their verdict becomes one written page: the starting line. Written down, no drift after.

Technical notes

The verdict became a single file, the starting line: winning hook, visual language, ordered project slate, three proof anchors, anti-patterns list. Everything after this point executes that page; a human gate approves each fork before anything is built.

03

gate.

A human approves every step. Models never get to approve their own work.

Technical notes

Three gate decisions in this build were changed or rejected by the owner mid-debate: the one-liner choice, the visual language, and the hero proof anchor (the provider-matrix hero was killed after a human critique about API-injection being unremarkable).

04

build.

One pass against the locked brief. No chat loops, no scope creep mid-build.

Technical notes

One model (claude-opus-5) built the entire page from the locked brief in a single pass: 26 KB of HTML, editorial-minimal, zero external dependencies. Truncated first attempts were detected (finish_reason) and re-fired; the thinking-budget trap was diagnosed and removed.

05

verify.

A bot tests the result like a picky user: viewport, timing, links, layout.

Technical notes

Browser automation (ego-browser) opened the page, toggled both color themes, checked the embedded topology for text overflow and iframe scrollbars, emulated a 390px mobile viewport, and asserted every link resolves. Verification is a machine's job; screenshots are kept as receipts.

06

ship and maintain.

Deterministic deploy, then a monthly ritual that feeds findings back into the next debate.

Technical notes

Deterministic deploy to Cloudflare Pages: byte-identical rebuilds, no surprise diffs. Monthly maintenance: metrics review, stale-evidence retirement, and findings fed back into the next debate round. The loop is the deliverable.
